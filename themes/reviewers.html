Reviewers

Who can we trust to grade work? Every audited reviewer, ranked by trust score, with the drop list called out.

What we measured

We sampled reviews from each reviewer and had an LLM auditor verify the claims in their feedback against the rubric + worker output. A reviewer's trust score is the share of their audits the auditor judged defensible.

What we found

36 of 40 audited reviewers cleared the trust bar. 4 were dropped for majority-unjustified verdicts. The remaining trusted pool produced 883 reviews that drive every downstream chart.

What to do

  • Review the dropped list (4 reviewers) and disable the accounts.
  • For borderline reviewers (trust 0.4–0.6), open the full audit cards in the audit evidence viewer (local-only).
  • Audit more reviewers — a chunk are still uncovered.

Behind the numbers

For each reviewer in our Goal-2 audit sample, an LLM auditor judges every claim in their quality_feedback text against the rubric + the worker's actual deliverable. Verdicts roll up to feedback_defensible, feedback_questionable, or feedback_unjustified at the review level.

  • Trust score = (n_defensible + 0.5 × n_questionable) / n_audited.
  • Drop rule: any reviewer whose majority of audits are feedback_unjustified.

Source: analysis/g4_reviewer_trust.csv, analysis/g4_dropped_reviewers.csv, analysis/g4_trusted_reviews.csv from analysis.run_g4 step-1 + analysis.lib.trust.

Things to watch for
  • The audit sample is small (~200 reviews from ~40 reviewers). 6 of 40 reviewers have N ≤ 5 audits each — a single new audit can swing trust by 20 percentage points.
  • Unaudited reviewers are kept by default. Roughly 30 reviewers project-wide have no audit yet.
  • Trust is a quality signal, not a fraud signal. A dropped reviewer may be well-intentioned but inaccurate; treat the drop list as 'do not weight their reviews for promotion gates' rather than 'punitive action'.
